Carving up the address space

One virtual network, four departments, and a rule most people meet the hard way: a subnet in Azure does not give you every address inside it. Allocate a prefix and a starting address to each department so that all four fit, none overlap, and each has room for the growth stated.

Virtual network 10.20.0.0/16, Miami office
10.20.0.010.20.64.010.20.128.010.20.192.010.20.255.255

Every department needs room for today plus the growth in its row.
Why the usable count is not what you expect

Azure takes five addresses out of every subnet before you get any. The first is the network address and the last is the broadcast address, which you would expect from any network. The other three are taken by the platform for the default gateway and for its own name resolution and configuration services.

So a /27 holds 32 addresses and gives you 27 machines. A department of 20 that expects to reach 30 does not fit, and the person who sized it did the arithmetic correctly for a physical network and got it wrong here. The smallest subnet permitted is a /29, which leaves three usable addresses.

A subnet cannot be resized once machines are attached to it without moving those machines, so the growth allowance is not a nicety. It is the difference between a change request and a migration.

Nothing is saved. The address plan here is an exercise, not our live network.
